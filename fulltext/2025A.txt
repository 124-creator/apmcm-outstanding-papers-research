A Multi-Objective Motion Planning and Energy Optimization 
Framework for Humanoid Robots in Highly Dynamic Scenarios 
summary 
The consumer-levelservicescenarios of robotics technology are rapidly expanding, and the 
motion performance, operationalstability, andsystem energy efficiency of humanoid robots in 
highly dynamic environments have become core challenges constraining their widespread 
deployment. Through establishing aseries of mathematical models, the core problems of 
motion planning,stability control, and energy optimization are addressed. 
Problem 1: Thisstudy established a kinematic model based on Denavit-Hartenberg 
parameters. Through forward kinematics calculation, the final coordinates of the arm end 
point were determined as (146.4, 84.5, 292.7) mm. Analysis demonstrates that the motion 
trajectory issmooth and continuous, and all motor parameters are withinsafe operating 
ranges, meeting the core requirements of robotstability and reliability. 
Problem 2: Asingle-leg joint kinematic model based on quintic polynomial 
trajectories was constructed for the robot'sstraight-line non-uniform velocity motion task. 
The motion process adopts a three-stage velocity curve with total time T=5.0s. The 
maximum knee joint angular velocity (0.4924 rad/s) occurs at t=0.611s and t=4.389s, 
which is far belowsystemsafety limits, ensuringsmooth motor operation. 
Problem 3: A multi-model fusion framework of "analytical motion planning + 
coordinated optimization control" was designed for the Unitree G1 humanoid robot's 
complex combined motion. Dual-arm joint trajectories were generated through inverse 
kinematics, and a waist-lower limb coordination optimizer was constructed tostabilize the 
center of mass projection within thesupport polygon, achieving modeling and verification of 
29 joint angle functions within the 0–4s time interval. 
Problem 4: Thisstudy established an energy consumption calculation model and 
constrained optimization model based on motor dynamics. By defining timescale factor and 
amplitudescale factors as optimization variables, thesequential Quadratic Programming 
(SQP) algorithm was adopted forsolving. Optimization resultsshow that all three problems 
achieved a 33.3% energy reduction with optimal timescale factors of 1.500. Comprehensive 
analysis visualization charts were generated, including energy comparison, joint energy 
breakdown, power-time curves, and optimization parameters, verifying the model's 
effectiveness and robustness acrosssystems of different complexity (from 2 joints to 21 joints), 
confirming that this optimization methodsignificantly reduces energy consumption without 
changing motion patterns. 
Key words:  Denavit-Hartenberg Model 、 Non-Uniform Velocity Model 、Multi-joint 
Cooperative Control 、 3D Trajectory Visualization、Constrained Optimization 、sequential 
Quadratic Programming

I. Introduction 
1.1  Background 
With the advancement of advanced technologies, robotics has become a frontier field. 
As robotic applications expand fromstructured industrialsettings to open, 
non-structured consumerservicescenarios, humanoid robots face core challenges in 
maintaining excellent motion performance,stability, and energy efficiency in highly 
dynamic environments. The Unitree G1 humanoid robot, developed by Hangzhou 
Unitree Robotics, integrates a multi-degree-of-freedom motionsystem with 
high-precision visual perception modules, providing an ideal platform forstudying 
comprehensive robot performance in complex tasks. 
However, during complex task execution, robotsstill confrontsignificant 
technical bottlenecks in the coordinated optimization of motorsafety control,stability 
management, and multi-joint coordinated planning. For example,sudden motor torque 
changes or load imbalances can easily lead to loss of control; improper weight 
distribution or trajectory planning can disrupt dynamic balance, causing falls; while 
highly complex motionsequences createsignificant energy consumption pressures, 
directly limiting the robot's operational duration and reliability. 
Therefore, thisstudy aims to establish precise mathematical models 
tosystematically optimize the robot's joint motion, trajectory planning, multi-body 
coordination, and energy consumption management, thereby enhancing thesafe and 
controllable operation and energy efficiency maximization of humanoid robots. 
1.2 Problem Restatement 
Based on the interpretation and analysis of robot performance optimization, the 
following problems need to be addressed: 
1.For thespecified motion of the robot's left arm, it is necessary to establish its 
kinematic model to calculate thespatial coordinates of the arm endpoint and verify 
whether this motion remains within thesafety operating limits of the joint motors. 
2.For the robot's non-uniform linear movement task, it is necessary to develop a 
kinematic model ofsingle-leg joint angles over time based on gait planning, calculate 
the time instant when the knee joint's angular velocity reaches its maximum, and 
determine the total time required to complete the movement. 
3.For the complex coordinated motion involving body rotation and circular arm 
movements, it is necessary to establish a multi-joint collaborative motion model that 
integrates upper-limb trajectory planning with lower-limb balance compensation, 
describing the angular variations of each joint throughout the complete motion cycle. 
4.Considering all the aforementioned performance actions collectively, it is 
necessary to construct an energy consumption assessment model based on joint torque 
and velocity to calculate the total energy consumption,subsequently propose an action 
optimization model targeting energy efficiency, and compare the energy consumption 
results before and after optimization.

2.1  Analysis ofspecific Issues  
2.1.1 Analysis of Problem 1 
Solve the forward kinematics for YuShu humanoid’s left arm:starting from the joint 
zero position, perform two motions—60° forward extension and 30° left 
rotation—with theshoulder as the origin. Using the DH model based on actual 
jointstructure, link parameters, and motorsafety limits, compute the final Cartesian 
coordinates of the arm endpoint and verify motorsafety. 
2.1.2 Analysis of Problem 2 
Develop a mathematical model for robot walking from (0,0,0) to (10,0,0) with 
non-uniform velocity. Model joint angles (hip, knee, ankle) over time. Find when 
knee angle changes most rapidly. Calculate total travel time. 
2.1.3 Analysis of Problem 3 
Problem Three: Unitree robot performs complex motion (0-4s): 45° torso rotation, 
dual arms drawing opposite circles, legs balancing. Challenges: multi-joint coupling, 
center of mass disturbance, waist-hip optimization. Model ensures precise timing, 
dynamic consistency, constraint feasibility, and 'rotate body first, then trigger legs' 
verification. 
2.1.4 Analysis of Problem 4 
Problem 4: Optimize energy via trajectoryscaling (time/amplitude) using Problems 
1-3 data. Challenges: 1) Accurate energy model linking joint kinematics (angle, vel, 
acc) to motor energy (torque, power). 2) Definescale factors, constrained optimization 
for feasible/safe motion. 3)solve withsQP for optimal parameters. 4) Visualize results 
(energy breakdown, power-time curves) for validation. Requires full pipeline 
(preprocessing, modeling, optimization, analysis) robust across 3-21 joints.

III. Basic assumption 
Assumptions are core to robot optimization modeling, transforming complex 
engineering problems intosolvable mathematical models. 
➢ Linkstructure Assumption: The left arm issimplified to 2 rigid links (upper arm 
+ forearm), ignoring flexiblestructuressuch as arm muscles andskin, retaining only 
geometric dimensions with upper arm length of 170 mm and forearm length of 168 
mm. 
➢ Independence Assumption: Theshoulder joint is responsible for forward lifting 
of 60° and left rotation of 30°, while the elbow joint is only responsible for forearm 
flexion and extension. Joint motions have no coupling interference. 
➢ Joint Trajectorysmoothness Assumption: Polynomial interpolation is used to 
generate joint angle-time curves, ensuring continuity of angle, velocity, and 
acceleration to avoid motorstart-stop impacts. 
➢ Motor Model Assumptions: Motor torque is proportional to joint angular 
acceleration. Motor power consists of dynamic power and base power. Moment of 
inertia and base power remain constant during motion. 
➢ Motion Assumptions: Joint angle trajectories can bescaled in both time and 
amplitude dimensions. Optimized trajectories maintain thesame motion pattern as 
original trajectories. 
➢ Optimization Assumptions: Timescale factor and amplitudescale factors have 
value ranges of [0.5, 1.5]. The optimization algorithm can converge to the global 
optimalsolution or asolution close to the global optimum.

the Denavit– Hartenberg (DH) model is employed tosystematically represent 
multi-joint coordinate transformations. Combined with the Yushu G1sDK Guide, this 
approach transforms joint motions into matrix operations for accurate coordinate 
computation and validates motionsafety using hardware parameters, thereby laying 
the groundwork for trajectory planning and multi-joint control insubsequent 
problems.

precise calculation of the endpoint coordinates for the Yushu humanoid robot's left 
arm. It begins with the robot's initial posture (all joints at zero degrees), following the 
problem requirements and establishing the physical andsafety guidelines of the Yushu 
G1 platform.

VII. Evaluation and Promotion of Model 
6.1  Strength And Weakness 
6.1.1 Strength 
(1) High computational efficiency andstrong verifiability: The model utilizes the 
established Denavit–Hartenberg (DH) parameter method, requiring no complex 
iterative computation. Coordinates are derived through directsubstitution of joint 
angles and link lengths, and results can be verified via geometric back-calculation. 
(2)Quantifiable non-uniform motion aligned with gait characteristics: Moving beyond 
asimplistic constant-velocity model, the motion is decomposed into acceleration, 
constant-speed, and deceleration phases, accurately described using a piecewise-defined 
function. 
(3)High computational efficiency: The analytical inverse kinematics method avoids iterative 
optimization, providing excellent real-time performance and enabling rapid generation of 
continuous time functions for all 29 joints, meeting real-time control requirements. 
6.1.2Weakness 
(1)Insufficient motionsmoothness: Due to joint limit clipping and the consideration of only 
2D planar motion, elbow joint angles exhibitsudden jumps at certain moments, resulting in 
discontinuous joint velocities that may affect the actual robot's motionsmoothness. 
(2)Local Optimal Risk:sQP algorithm may converge to local optimalsolutions rather than 
global optimalsolutions, and optimization results may not be theoretically optimal.
