The unit commitment (UC) problem in power systems entails high computational complex-
ity due to its mixed-integer nature and stringent operational requirements. This work develops a
classicalmodelincorporatingnetworkandsecurityconstraints,thenreformulatesitasa quadratic
unconstrained binary optimisation (QUBO)modelcompatiblewithemergingquantumhardware.
A quantum heuristic algorithm,combinedwithatailored scale-reduction strategy,validatesthe
feasibilityofquantum-inspiredoptimisationforpracticaldispatchapplications.
For Problem One, a mixed-integer programming (MIP) model is solved via Gurobi for
24-hour economic scheduling. The formulation includes fuel and start-up costs, physical genera-
tion limits, ramping capabilities, and minimum up/down times, with logical unit status constraints
rigorously enforced using the Big M method. Results show Unit 1 supplies base load at 263.9
MW,whileUnit2startsinperiod6at 49.5 MW.Totalsystemcostdecreasesfrom $5,029(16:00)
to $2,784(24:00), with perfect power balance maintained across all periods and switching actions
minimised,confirmingstrongeconomicandoperationalperformance.
For Problem Two, an extended mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model inte-
grates piecewise-linearised generation costs, DC power flow equations, N-1 contingency se-
curity, and spinning reserve requirements into a unified framework. Solved efficiently with a
commercial solver, it yields a total operating cost of £191,789.67 (including £639.55 in start-up
costs)andanaverageof 4.52 committed units. Alltransmissionlineflowsremainwithinthermal
limits while satisfying reserve margins, demonstrating the modelns capability to enhance system
robustnesswithoutsacrificingcostefficiency.
For Problem Three, a QUBO model is constructed via binary discretisation of generation
variables. Operational and network constraintsiincluding power balance, ramping, min up/down
times,DCflowlimits,N-1security,andspinningreserveiareembeddedusinga penalty function
approach. Solved with the KaiwuSDK quantum heuristic solver, it yields costs of $21,850(Unit
1) and $19,640 (Unit 2), with outputs deviating by less than 2.5% from classical MILP solutions
andaminimumruntimeof 5.6 s,demonstratingstrongpotentialfornear-real-timedispatch.
For Problem Four, a hierarchical scale-reduction framework based on K-means cluster-
ing of load profiles, electrical similarity-based unit aggregation,and transmission distribution
factor screeningeffectivelycompressestheQUBOproblemsize. Themethodachievesamaximum
dimensionalityreductionof 48%,yieldingacompactmodelofonly 520 bits,solvedin 9.1 seconds
with total cost increase strictly controlled within 15%. This confirms that strategic problem com-
pressioncanpreservesolutionqualitywhilealigningwithcurrentquantumhardwarelimitations.
This work establishes a hybrid classical–quantum UC frameworkthat addresses economic
dispatchwithnetworkandsecurityconstraints,bridgingtheoreticalquantumadvantageandpractical
powersystemneedsthroughintelligenttransformationandscalableencoding.

eratorschedulestominimisecostwhilemeetingdemandandoperationalconstraints. Itsexponential

IX. Advantages and Limitations of the Model
9 . 1 Advantages of the Model
⊙ Themodelpreciselycapturesunitstart-up/shutdownlogicandoutputconstraintsthroughmixed-
integer programming, effectively handling both discrete and continuous decision variables to
ensure dispatch schemes align with operational requirements. Segmented linearisation tech-
niques are employed to address non-linear cost functions, enhancing solution efficiency and
computationalstability.
⊙ TheincorporationofDCpowerflowandN-1securityconstraintsenhancesthemodel’sadapt-
ability to transmission bottlenecks and fault scenarios within the grid. Key line coupling re-
lationships are identified through power transfer distribution factors, significantly reducing
computationalcomplexitywhilemaintainingsecurity.
9 . 2 Model Limitations
The model exhibits high sensitivity to initial parameters, with improper data preprocessing
readily yielding infeasible or suboptimal solutions. The increased complexity of constraints ex-
acerbates computational demands, particularly in high-dimensional fault scenarios where solution
timesescalatesignificantly. Whilstquantumreductionstrategiesdiminishvariablescale,theymay
compromiseeconomicprecision,therebyaffectingpracticalapplicability.
