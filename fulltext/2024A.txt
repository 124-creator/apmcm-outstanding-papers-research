A Multi-compound Model for Underwater Image Enhancement Based
on Physical Theorem and Machine learning
Abstract
The high cost and low tolerance for error in underwater photography have made salvaging degraded underwater
images a signiﬁcant challenge for marine researchers, underwater tourism enthusiasts, and others. Therefore,
we leverage physical theorems and machine learning to conduct multidimensional classiﬁcation, cause analysis,
and enhancement restoration of degraded underwater images.
For Question 1: Firstly, we observed the causes of degradation in images, resulting in a visibility ranking
from Low Light to Color Cast, and then to Blur. The categorization priority was thus established. Subse-
quently, a grayscale histogram analysis model was developed to determine whether an image is underexposed;
an RGB histogram model combined with Jensen-Shannon divergence was employed to assess color cast;
and the Laplacian operator was utilized to analyze image blurriness. Ultimately, in Appendix 1, 33 images
were identiﬁed as blurry, 215 as color-cast, 22 as low-light, and 130 as normal.
For Question 2: According to the Jaﬀe-McGlamery imaging model, we put forward a simpliﬁed and
revised underwater image model. Then, we decomposed the formula to cope with single degradation case
and came up with underwater image degradation models, such as the color cast model, the low-light model,
and the clarity model. In the end, we preprocessed the attachment 1 dataset with an innovate statistical
method Noise Adversarial Preprocessing (NAP). Increasingly, we used a algorithm based on methods
of least square and exponential smoothing to obtain the approximate value of 𝛽𝐷 𝑐 and 𝛽𝐵 𝑐 , analyzed the
rationality and the outlier data and pointed out the similarities and diﬀerence between these models.
For Question 3: For blurred images, we propose a strategy of denoising ﬁrst, followed by sharpening,
and ﬁnally enhancing contrast. For color-cast images, we establish a random forest model and extend the
dataset using the Forward-backward theory, applying the training results to correct the images. For low-light
images, we employ a grayscale adjustment method. Subsequently, PSNR, UCIQE, and UIQM validated
the excellence of the comprehensive strategy and grayscale adjustment. Meanwhile, we analyzed the reasons
behind the insensitivity of the FbRF model to UCIQE.
For Question 4 & 5: We analyzed the drawbacks of current methods of underwater image enhancement,
and shed light upon the need for our improved Multi-compound Model with a Multi-scale 𝑁-fold Fusion
techniques. After introduce its speciﬁc steps, we applied it to the pictures in attachment 2, which showcases the
beneﬁts of our model.Additionally, SIFT(Scale-Invariant Feature Transform) is introduced to better judge
the image quality. Then we put forward feasible suggestions of underwater optical enhancement tailored for
speciﬁc and complex scenarios.
Finally, we analyzed the sensitivity of theorem in Question 2 and the robustness of our multi-compound
model. Then we discussed the limitations of our model and the prospects for future research.
Keywords: Grayscale RGB&Lab Color Space Laplacian Operator Multi-compound Model Multi-scale
𝑁-fold Fusion Techniques Randon Forest Model Forward-backward Theory SIFT Keypoint

1 Introduction
1 . 1 Problem Background
Images serve as a critical medium for transmitting substantial information through visual representation. In
contemporary marine scientiﬁc research, particularly in areas such as deep-sea topography surveying and
seabed resource investigation, underwater imagery plays an indispensable role. Moreover, underwater images
hold invaluable signiﬁcance as precious memories and mementos for many marine tourism enthusiasts. They
exhibit a pronounced demand and aspiration for high-quality images.
However, underwater conditions are complex and dynamic, with the shooting environment often un-
controllable. Numerous factors can cause catastrophic degradation of image quality. For instance, as depth
increases, light rapidly attenuates, resulting in dim images. Particles, plankton, and pollutants in the water
can interfere with light propagation, leading to blurriness or distortion in underwater images. The color of
underwater scenes can vary depending on depth and water type. Additionally, water currents, waves, or motion
of objects can induce camera or subject shake, aﬀecting image stability and clarity, among other issues.
Therefore, the establishment of a model capable of enhancing the quality of underwater images is of
paramount importance.

1 . 2 Restatement of the Problem
Considering the background information and restricted conditions identiﬁed in the problem statement, we need
to solve the following problems:
1. For the provided set of underwater images, image analysis and statistical techniques should be employed
to develop a multi-dimensional analytical model, with the images classiﬁed into three categories: color
cast, low light, and blur, while the reasons behind each classiﬁcation should be elucidated.
2. For underwater images with diﬀerent types of degradation, an underwater scene image degradation model
should be established using the given underwater imaging model. Additionally, the similarities and
diﬀerences in the causes of degradation under diﬀerent scenarios should be analyzed, and the distinctions
among various degradation models should be compared.
3. For underwater images with a single degradation cause, a targeted image enhancement solution should
be proposed. Image quality assessment metrics such as PSNR, UCIQE [9] , and UIQM [5] should be
used to establish an evaluation model, and a quantitative assessment of the obtained enhancement results
should be conducted.

The physical model of underwater image degradation is applicable to most underwater scenarios, such
as shallow water, deep water, and turbid water. Although the degradation degree may vary across
diﬀerent environments, the fundamental principles (such as light absorption and scattering eﬀects) can be
eﬀectively described in the model by adjusting the parameters.
• Forward scattering Light Removal Assumption
The assumption of not considering the complete removal of forward scattering light is not applicable
in high turbidity water with strong scattering eﬀects. However, this kind of situation is not common
underwater, so when the camera and the object are close enough, the eﬀect of forwarding scattered light
is reasonable to remove in the Jafe-McGlamery imaging theory.
• Smoothed Illumination Scene Prior Assumption
Due to the inﬂuence of non-uniform illumination under complex lighting conditions, underwater images
have a higher probability of non-uniform color decay and high brightness areas, which is easy to cause
problems such as over-enhancement of local areas and loss of details. Considering the unpredictability
and chaos of complex lighting scenes, it is diﬃcult to simulate the underwater imaging problem with
physical and mathematical models, so we mainly consider the attenuation of natural light in the water
without occlusion, and also forget the case of artiﬁcial ﬁlling light.

4 . 1 . 3 Jensen-Shannon-divergence-based RGB Histogram Model
In the RGB histogram, the X-axis represents the color intensity of each channel (ranging from 0 to 255), while
the Y-axis represents the frequency at which each intensity value occurs. It illustrates the distribution of each
color channel (Red R, Green G, Blue B).
In Figure 5, we can observe that color cast in the RGB histogram typically manifests as one channel
deviating from the others. The greater the overlap between this channel and the other channels, the larger the
portion of the color represented by this channel at a speciﬁc pixel intensity, which results in a higher degree of
color cast in the image.
Therefore, we introduce the Jensen-Shannon divergence to quantitatively evaluate the degree of disparity
between the color channels.

When light travels underwater, pure water and impurities in the water will cause the light to scatter, making
some of the light deviate from the incident direction. Underwater scattering can be divided into two types:
forward scatter and backward scatter.
In the famous Jaﬀe-McGlamery imaging mode[6], underwater images are usually represented by the
superposition of a linear combination of three components, which are direct component, backward scattering
component and forward scattering component. The forward scattering component mainly causes the loss of
image details, and the back scattering component mainly causes the lack of image contrast and image blur.
Compared with backscatter and forward scatter, backscatter usually plays a more important role in underwater
image imaging. Due to the serious scattering of light underwater, underwater images will present serious
problems such as low contrast, blurred edge details and signiﬁcant noise as the shooting distance becomes
longer. Usually, the imaging eﬀect is better when the distance taken underwater is less than 10 meters. The
illustration of the underwater image formation model is shown in Figure 9.
Figure 9 Schematic of the Underwater Optical Imaging Model

4 . 2 . 2 Underwater Image Degradation Model
Underwater optical properties can be divided into two types: intrinsic optical properties [4] and expressive
optical properties, which are only related to the intrinsic properties of the water medium,or related to both the
intrinsic properties of the water medium and the distribution of the surrounding light ﬁeld, respectively. In
the universal situation, the distribution and structure of the light ﬁeld are diﬃcult to be measured, so when
studying the underwater optical characteristics, we only study the intrinsic optical characteristics ,such as the
depth of objects, and the absorption and scattering eﬀect of the water medium on light.
On land, light is transmitted through air. But when we shoot underwater, the medium becomes water.
When light moves underwater, it will be absorbed and scattered by suspended particles in the water. Absorption
causes attenuation of light energy, and of course, scattering causes changes in the direction of light propagation.
The attenuation of light under water is mainly caused by the absorption and scattering of light by the medium in
the water, and the attenuation degree also changes with the change of distance. Figure 10 shows the attenuation

4 . 3 . 1 Fusion Enhancement Strategy Tailored for Blurred Scenarios
For blurry images, we propose a three-step enhancement scheme. As shown in Figure 13, the image is ﬁrst
denoised, followed by sharpening, and ﬁnally, a comprehensive contrast enhancement is applied. This is the
optimal sequence derived from repeated experiments.
• Denois : Gaussian ﬁltering is used to remove noise from the image, making it smoother and preventing
noise from interfering with sharpening or contrast enhancement. However, denoising may lead to the
loss of details. As observed in Figure 13, after the denoising process, the Laplacian value of the image
decreases.
• Sharpen : The custom sharpening convolution kernel serves as a sharpening ﬁlter, enhancing pixel
details while reducing the inﬂuence of surrounding pixels on the image. This helps to emphasize the
edges, making the image appear clearer. Sharpening enhances the edges, but if the image already contains
signiﬁcant noise, the sharpening process can also amplify the noise. Therefore, the denoising step must
be performed eﬀectively to ensure that sharpening does not exacerbate the noise issue.
• Contrast Enhance : CLAHE (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) is used to improve
the contrast of the image, enhancing its visual impact by making bright areas brighter and dark areas
darker, thereby making details more prominent. This method adaptively adjusts the contrast in localized
regions of the image, ensuring that over-enhancement or excessive noise ampliﬁcation in uniform regions
is avoided.

4 . 4 Question 4: Propose Multi-Compound Underwater Image Enhancement Model
4 . 4 . 1 The Need for a Multi-Compound Model
Underwater optical image enhancement aims to eliminate the inﬂuence of absorption and scattering, remove
color cast, improve contrast and enhance image details through some image processing techniques.
At present, the research on underwater optical image enhancement methods focuses on the above problems,
including traditional methods based on physical models, traditional methods based on non-physical models,
and methods based on deep learning. Traditional underwater optical image enhancement methods without
physical model do not consider the image degradation process, and directly adjust the image pixels, which lacks
certain theoretical basis and is easy to cause problems such as image color distortion and over-enhancement.
The traditional underwater optical image enhancement method based on physical model has certain theoretical
support for mathematical modeling of underwater optical image degradation process, but it is highly dependent
on the imaging model. The traditional imaging model has the limitation of incomplete description of underwater
optical image degradation process, resulting in incomplete color deviation correction.
The underwater optical image enhancement method based on machine learning automatically learns the
mapping relationship between high-quality underwater images and low-quality ones by using the powerful
feature learning and nonlinear ﬁtting ability of network to realize underwater image enhancement. Such
methods can reduce the inﬂuence of complex underwater environment on the results through a large number of
sample learning, but they are highly dependent on the training data set, and often suﬀer from poor robustness.
4 . 4 . 2 Our Multi-Compound Model
For our Multi-Compounded Model, it not only tackles with the issue that traditional methods have estimating
the imaging physical model parameters, but also the transmission map parameter estimation method in the
original physical model is improved, and the physical model is combined with the machine learning , so that
the machine learning method has the principle support of underwater optical imaging, and the dependence of
the recovery eﬀect of the machine learning method on the training data set is reduced.

4 . 5 Question 5: Propose Feasibility Suggestions
Traditional underwater optical image enhancement methods based on physical principles, although having
strong reliability, clear theories, and relatively saving computational resources, are basically veriﬁed in labora-
tory environments, lacking experimental veriﬁcation in real outdoor underwater scenes and thus lacking certain
practicability. Some methods are highly dependent on parameter estimation and have ill-posed problems in
complex and changeable water environments. For example, the Low-light Model we used before, the method
using orthogonal polarization diﬀerence proposed by Wang [7], and the method of backscattered pixel prior
proposed by Zhou [10].
The underwater optical image enhancement methods based on deep learning, although able to automat-
ically learn the mapping relationship between high-quality underwater images and low-quality underwater
images through a large number of sample learning, are highly dependent on the training data set, have high
requirements for the quality of the training data set, require large computational power, and small underwater
cameras or detectors often cannot meet the requirements at present, and often have the problem of poor ro-
bustness. For example, the Random Forest Model we used before, the method of pyramid network structure,
LCNet, based on CNN proposed by Jiang [3] , and the method of multi-scale generator and dual discriminator
structure based on GAN proposed by Yang [8].
Therefore, we put forward the following suggestions:
1. In actual underwater scenes, the methods based on physical principles should be combined with those
based on deep learning according to the actual situation to balance the computational power problem and
the ability to deal with complex situations.
2. Use multiple algorithms to improve the model’s ability to deal with diﬀerent image degradation scenarios.
3. Improve the level of hardware perfection, use multimodal sensors to obtain scene information, explore
the diﬀerent feature distributions of underwater multimodal information, analyze the correlation and
complementarity between modalities, and eﬀectively fuse the multimodal information to further improve
the underwater image quality.

6 Strengths and Weaknesses
6 . 1 Analysis of Strengths
1. The algorithm has a good ability for continuous optimization. The color diﬀerence reduction algorithm
among our three core algorithms is based on the random forest algorithm, and the amount of data in
the database can be further expanded to improve the code’s performance. Due to time constraints, it is
diﬃcult to collect more pictures as the training set, so the algorithm cannot be perfect, but it still has great
potential and can achieve better restoration results.
2. The algorithm has a wide range of applications. This algorithm not only has a high restoration eﬀect
on underwater pictures, but also has a good restoration eﬀect on various problems such as external color
pollution, high blurriness, and low brightness. When solving the problem of underwater image restoration,
we ﬁrst extracted the main problems existing in the underwater image, then solved the individual problems
one by one, and ﬁnally solved the multi-problem images through methods such as joint processing and
image fusion. Such a method enables the algorithm to be widely applied to the restoration of other
polluted pictures.
3. The algorithm has good universality. The algorithm only uses the physical model constructed based on
underwater images and the machine learning method of random forest, and cleverly alternately uses single-
factor processing and image fusion on the image to repair the image in complex situations. It avoids using

6 . 2 Analysis of Weaknesses
1. The restoration ability for backgrounds such as seawater is inferior to that for objects. Since the color
of the seawater and other environments is relatively light and is easily interfered by external colors, it is
diﬃcult to judge its original color. Therefore, it can only reduce the degree of external color interference,
but cannot directly restore the original color of the object. In the future, eﬀorts can be made to separate
the background and object, and better restoration results can be achieved by processing the background
separately.
2. Although the degree of image restoration is relatively high, the restoration still starts from the overall pic-
ture. In actual life, the restoration methods for diﬀerent objects should be slightly diﬀerent. Subsequently,
the code can be upgraded in this regard. First, the objects in the picture are separated, and each object is
restored separately and then the overall picture is recombined. In this way, the restoration of each part in
the picture will be more in place.
